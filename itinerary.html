5–8 October · Brianza & Lake Como

Base: HSM Hotel San Martino, Garbagnate Monastero (LC). 3★, 24-hour reception, free parking, good breakfast — but no proper restaurant, and it sits ~500 m off the SS36, so ask for a room away from the road. It's a base, not a destination; everything good is 20–50 min away by car.

Mon 5 Oct

Land, drive, sleep

MXP 19:00 → Garbagnate Monastero · ~85 km · sunset 18:58

  • 19:00Wheels down at Malpensa.
  • 19:20–20:15Passport control and bags. Allow the full 45 minutes — evening T1 queues are real.
  • 20:15–21:00Rental car. The majors are in the multi-storey directly connected to T1 arrivals; brokers need a shuttle, which adds 20–30 min.
  • ~21:00Eat here, at the airport, before you drive. Landside T1 runs to ~22:00–23:00.
  • 21:20Drive: A8 → Tangenziale Nord → SS36 north → exit Nibionno / Costa Masnaga. After 20:00 Milan is clear. ~1h15.
  • ~22:30Check in. 24-hour reception. A drink at the hotel bar and bed.
Don't plan a restaurant dinner tonight. You'll realistically be on the road at 21:00, Italian kitchens in Brianza take last orders 21:30–22:00, and Monday is the standard closing day for restaurants here. Eating at Malpensa is unromantic but it's the difference between arriving fed and arriving at 22:30 hunting for an open kitchen. As a hedge, have the hotel ring Birra & Brace (roadside grill, 2 min away, +39 031 851700 via reception) that afternoon.
Toll warning: if the satnav routes you onto the A36 Pedemontana, there are no toll booths — it bills by licence plate and must be paid online within 15 days, or the rental company adds an admin fee months later. Avoid it, or pay at pedemontana.com.

Tue 6 Oct

West shore — Balbianello & Carlotta

Lenno & Tremezzo → Bellagio by ferry · ~1h10 each way

The order of these two days is not negotiable. Villa del Balbianello is closed Mondays and Wednesdays; Villa Monastero in Varenna is closed Tuesdays. So: west shore today, Varenna tomorrow. Reverse it and you lose both headline villas.

  • 08:30Drive to Lenno via Como (~1h10).
  • 10:00Villa del Balbianello — the promontory villa from Casino Royale and Star Wars. Gardens ~€13–14; gardens + 45-min interior tour ~€24–25. Get there by taxi-boat from Lido di Lenno (~€6 each way, 5 min) or the woodland path (20–25 min, stony, uphill).
  • 12:30Lunch in Lenno or Tremezzo, on the water.
  • 14:30Villa Carlotta, Tremezzo — 7 hectares of botanic garden plus Canova and Hayez inside. Open daily, 10:00–19:00 until 18 Oct. ~€15–17.50.
  • 17:00Ferry Cadenabbia → Bellagio as foot passengers. Villa Melzi gardens (€10, to 19:00) or just the lanes and the lakefront.
  • 18:00Golden hour is 17:45–18:55. If you do one boat trip all trip, make it a private sunset hour from Bellagio or Varenna (~€150–200 for 1h, or shared from ~€40 pp). October light on this lake is the best of the year.
Ferry timetable: the summer schedule expires 4 October — the day before you arrive — and the autumn one wasn't published when I looked. Re-check navigazionelaghi.it two weeks out. Even the sparsest winter timetable runs roughly hourly 06:00–21:00, so you'll be fine, just confirm. The central-lake day pass (~€17.50) beats singles after three hops.
Parking Park once, then walk and use the shore bus. Lenno → Tremezzo → Cadenabbia is 3 km along one road, and the C10 bus runs that shore all day for a couple of euros. Moving the car three times is the classic wasted afternoon here.
  • Balbianello: aim for the Lido di Lenno lakefront lot — it's where the taxi-boat leaves from and where the woodland path starts. Paid, blue lines, roughly €1.50–2/h. Set the satnav to "Lido di Lenno", not the villa.
  • Free alternative: the residential streets uphill off the SS340 in Lenno and Mezzegra — white lines, 10–15 min walk down. Read the signs; anything yellow is residents-only.
  • Villa Carlotta: the small lot opposite the entrance fills fast. Better to leave the car at Lenno and take the C10 two stops, or park on the Tremezzo lakefront / near the Cadenabbia ferry pier and walk 10 min.
  • Ferry: leave the car at Cadenabbia and walk on. Don't put it on the car ferry — you don't need it in Bellagio and it costs several times a foot ticket.

Wed 7 Oct

Varenna — the old mule track

Sentiero del Viandante + Villa Monastero · ~50 min each way

  • 08:30Drive to Varenna (~50 min via SS36 through Lecco). Park at the Varenna-Esino-Perledo railway station — see below, it's the trick that makes today work.
  • 09:30Sentiero del Viandante, Varenna → Bellano. Ancient mule track high above the lake through chestnut woods. ~3h, ~530 m cumulative ascent, well waymarked. Start at Olivedo, up Via della Croce → Vezio → Regolo → the "Bosco delle Streghe" → Bellano. This is the best half-day walk of the Como leg.
  • 12:45Orrido di Bellano — a lit walkway bolted into a gorge carved by the Pioverna. €8. Then lunch in Bellano.
  • 14:30Train Bellano → Varenna, a few minutes, ~€2–3, frequent. Clean point-to-point walk, no backtracking.
  • 15:15Villa Monastero (open Wed; closed Tue) — a botanic garden strung a kilometre along the shore, plus the house-museum. Garden + museum €15, garden only €13. Last entry 17:30.
  • 19:30Dinner in Varenna — the prettiest village on the lake after dark. Il Cavatappi (tiny, stone alley, the romantic one) or Vecchia Varenna (on the water, in a former black-marble works).
Parking Park at the Varenna-Esino-Perledo train station, not down in the village. It sits on the hillside above Varenna with roadside and lot parking that's free or near-free, and it's a 10-minute walk down into the centre. And because you're returning from Bellano by train to that exact station, the car is waiting where you finish — no backtracking, no second parking payment, no walking back uphill at the end of a 3-hour hike.
  • If it's full: the terraced lots on the SP72 above the village and the ferry-pier lots at Olivedo are the paid options — blue lines, roughly €2/h or €12–15/day in high season, usually cheaper and less strictly enforced in October. Check the sign.
  • Free: roadside white lines up towards Perledo, the village above Varenna — 10–15 min down, 20–25 min back up.
  • Never drive into Varenna's centre. It's a restricted zone and the lanes are stairs and alleys — physically not driveable.
  • You don't need parking in Bellano; you arrive on foot and leave by train.
Alternative if the weather turns: the Lecco Wednesday market runs 07:30–14:00 in Piazza Garibaldi, 50+ stalls. Then Villa Manzoni (the house of the man who wrote Italy's national novel about this shore) and Palazzo delle Paure, €6 each, and the lakefront promenade under the Resegone. Parking in Lecco: the lakefront lots along Lungolario Isonzo are paid blue-line; the free white-line streets start ~10 min back from the water.

Thu 8 Oct

Over to the Dolomites, via Bergamo

Garbagnate Monastero → Selva di Val Gardena · ~340 km · 4h–4h45 driving

This is the longest drive of the trip. Break it in Bergamo, which sits directly on the A4 about 45 minutes in — so the stop costs you almost nothing in time.

  • 07:30Optional: a short walk on Monte Barro, 15 min from the hotel — sentiero 313 is an easy hour to a terrace over Lecco and the three Brianza lakes. Or drive straight to the Eremo for the view with no effort.
  • 10:00On the road. SS36 south → A4 east.
  • 11:00Bergamo Città Alta. Park in the lower town, funicular up (~€3 return, every 5–10 min). Piazza Vecchia, the Cappella Colleoni, the Venetian walls. Lunch: casoncelli alla bergamasca — this is a genuinely excellent food city.
  • 13:30A4 → A22 Brennero north → exit Chiusa/Klausen → SS242 up Val Gardena. Toll ~€21–25 total for the day.
  • ~17:00Check in at Selva. Dinner at Nives on the village square — confirmed open year-round, which in this valley in October is not a small thing.
Parking Monte Barro (free). On a weekday you can drive straight up to Piazzale Cappello degli Alpini and park free at the trailhead. The Eremo di Monte Barro also has free parking with a terrace view for zero effort. The €5 shuttle from the Fornace lot only runs on busy weekends — a non-issue on a Thursday.
  • Bergamo — free park-and-ride: Fiera di Bergamo has 300 free spaces served by bus line 1 into the centre, and Ex Bonini (Via San Bernardino 149) holds ~150 cars on line 6. Both are the cheap answer if you don't mind a bus.
  • Bergamo — paid, closer: ATB run Ex Gasometro (312 spaces, between Via Gavazzeni and Via San Giovanni Bosco), Via Baschenis (71 spaces) and the underground Stadio Atalanta at Piazzale Goisis (348 spaces, max height 2.20 m). All open 24/7. From any of them it's a short walk or one bus stop to the funicular base on Viale Vittorio Emanuele II.
  • Never drive up to Città Alta. It's a ZTL, Lombardy adds regional emissions restrictions on top, and parking up there is miserable even for those allowed in. Funicular, always — ~€3 return, every 5–10 min.

Fri 9 Oct

Seceda — the ridge everyone comes forFriends' pick

Seceda ridgeline → Rifugio Firenze → Col Raiser · 8.9 km, +575 m, 3–3.5 h, easy–moderate

Drive to Ortisei (park at the cable-car station — the village centre is one of the longest pedestrian zones in the Dolomites). Ortisei–Furnes–Seceda cable car up, ~€74 return, first up 08:30.

Walk the ridge under the Odle's tilted blade of rock, then drop to Rifugio Firenze / Regensburgerhütte for lunch — it closes 12 October, so this is one of your last chances. Lunch service 11:30–15:00. Descend to Col Raiser and gondola down to Santa Cristina.

Also open for lunch: Baita Sofie on Seceda (to 2 Nov) and Malga Odles (to 2 Nov).

Both cheaper Seceda routes you found rely on the Fermeda chairlift — and Fermeda closes 20 September 2026. It runs 20 June–20 September, so on 9 October it will have been shut for nearly three weeks. Any blog route that says "Col Raiser gondola → Fermeda chairlift → ridge", or "Cristauta/Praplan → trail 4 → Fermeda", is describing a summer trip. The good news is that the idea behind them still works — you just walk the Fermeda section.

The cheap, quiet version that does work in October. Drive to Santa Cristina and take the Col Raiser gondola instead of the Ortisei–Seceda cable car. Col Raiser runs to 1 November, costs materially less than €74 (I couldn't pull the 2026 tariff — check at the ticket window), and carries a fraction of the traffic. From the top station at ~2,106 m, walk up to the Seceda ridgeline via the Pieralongia rock towers — roughly +400 m, 1h15–1h30, which is exactly the bit the Fermeda chairlift used to do for you. Come back down past Rifugio Firenze for lunch. Same ridge, same view, less money, far fewer people, one extra hour of climbing.

The fully-on-foot version from the Cristauta / Praplan parking above Santa Cristina, up trail 4, is genuine but big — around +900–1,000 m to the ridge with no lift shortcut now that Fermeda is shut. Worth it if you want the climb; not if you want a relaxed first day in the mountains.

Which to pick: if the €74 doesn't bother you, the Ortisei cable car is the fastest way to the most famous view and gets you up in morning light. If you'd rather have the ridge with fewer people in it, take Col Raiser and walk the extra 400 m — that's the better honeymoon version. Either way go early; the Ortisei queue is the one genuine crowd of your Val Gardena leg, even in October.

Sat 10 Oct

Val di Funes, then TörggelenFriends' pick

Odle north faces in the morning, a farm table in the afternoon · both in Val d'Isarco, 15 min apart

Morning — Val di Funes. ~50 min from Selva, over the back of the same mountains you'll have walked on Seceda the day before. Park at Zannes / Zans at the head of the valley and walk the Adolf Munkel Weg — 9.2 km circuit, +378 m, 3–3.5 h, easy to moderate, running right along the base of the Odle's north wall. Huts on the route: Dusler Alm, Geisler Alm and Gschnagenhardt Alm.

On Geisler Alm being a "no go": I'd push back gently. Rifugio delle Odle / Geisler Alm sits directly on the Adolf Munkel Weg, so you walk past its door whether you stop or not — it costs you nothing to look. It has a reputation for being busy and a bit commercial in high summer, which is probably what the blog was reacting to; on a Saturday in mid-October that objection largely evaporates. The real risk is that it's shut — I couldn't verify its October season. Treat it as a bonus if the door's open, and carry something in your pack either way.

The postcard is the little church of Santa Maddalena with the Odle behind it. Non-resident cars are barred from the panorama viewpoint May–November — park in the village and walk 20–30 min. The Zannes car park is pre-bookable online in 2026; check before you drive up.

Shorter version if you'd rather protect the afternoon: skip Munkel, do the Santa Maddalena viewpoint walk and a short loop from Zannes — about 90 minutes total.

South Tyrol's "fifth season", from Torggl, the wooden wine press. You walk up to a licensed farm — a Buschenschank — and eat a long, communal, multi-course farm meal washed down with the year's brand-new, still-cloudy wine (Nuier) or unfermented must (Siaßer).

What arrives: roast chestnuts, barley soup, Schlutzkrapfen (spinach-ricotta ravioli), Knödel with butter-roasted potatoes, the Schlachtplatte of cured sausage and ham on sauerkraut, then Krapfen, Strauben and chestnut cake, finished with pine schnapps.

Where. Villandro / Villanders is the classic Törggelen village and its three certified farms — Oberpartegger, Winklerhof, Pschnickerhof — are linked by walking trails, so you can walk between them. Also excellent: Rielingerhof and Patscheiderhof on the Renon plateau above Bolzano, and Fronthof at Aica di Fiè, the closest to you.

Drink the Valle Isarco whites with it — Sylvaner, Kerner, Veltliner. They're built for this food. If you want a proper cellar visit instead of the farm route, Cantina Tramin at Termeno does shop tastings without booking, Sat 09:00–17:00 — but it's an hour further south and hard to combine with Funes.

Why these two go together: Zannes and Villandro are about 15 minutes apart at the top of the same valley. Hike under the Odle 09:30–12:30, drive down, sit at the farm table at 13:30. You end the day having done the best short hike and the best meal in South Tyrol without doubling back once.
Phone ahead. Törggelen nominally starts at the beginning of October but individual farms often don't open until mid-month, and you'd be driving an hour. Confirm before you go. Choose one farm and commit — this is a three-hour lunch, not a crawl. And whoever drives, doesn't drink; Italy's limit is 0.5 g/l and enforced.

Sun 11 Oct

The coffin gondola, then Alpe di SiusiBlog + friends' pick

Passo Sella morning · Alpe di Siusi afternoon · the most date-critical day of the trip

Today is your only shot at the "coffin gondola". The Forcella Sassolungo lift at Passo Sella — the two-person standing cabins that never stop, so you step in while it's moving — closes on 11 October 2026. You drive over Passo Sella the very next morning on your way to Cortina and it will already be shut. So it moves to today, or it doesn't happen.

One caveat: that 11 October date came from a single secondary source. Everything else about it fits the pattern (second Sunday of the month, same as Ciampinoi and Resciesa), but call or check before you build the morning around it.
  • 09:00Drive to Passo Sella — 20 min from Selva, free parking at the pass.
  • 09:30Forcella Sassolungo gondola up to Rifugio Toni Demetz at 2,681 m, wedged in the notch between the Sassolungo and the Cinque Dita. A five-minute ride, genuinely unnerving, and the view down into the cirque at the top is out of proportion to the effort. Coffee on the terrace, back down by ~11:00. The rifugio's own closing date isn't published — it normally tracks the lift.
  • 11:30Drive to Ortisei (~25 min) and take the Mont Sëuc gondola up to Alpe di Siusi. It runs to 2 November, so unlike everything else today it isn't going anywhere.

Alpe di Siusi, afternoon — up to 14.4 km, +483 m, 4.5 h, easy; or a much shorter loop from the top station if the morning has eaten your time. Rolling golden meadow with Sassolungo and the Sciliar on either side, and the low afternoon light is when it looks best. Lunch still available at Rifugio Sassopiatto (to 18 Oct), Rifugio Alpe di Tires (to 19 Oct), Malga Schgaguler (to 1 Nov), Rifugio Bolzano/Schlernhaus and Malga Gstatsch (both last day today).

Why not the Seiser Alm Bahn from Siusi today: it's the simpler option (€30 return) but it's on the wrong side of the massif from Passo Sella. Coming from the coffin gondola, Ortisei is on your way. Either lift gets you to the same meadow. Don't drive up — the Compatsch road is closed to non-resident cars uphill 09:00–17:00 and parking needs an online reservation.
Today is also the last day for Ciampinoi, Resciesa and Mont de Sëura. You can't do everything — the coffin gondola is the one worth protecting, since the other three have no equivalent anywhere else on your trip and Alpe di Siusi stays open for weeks.
Sunset detour: drive up to Passo Gardena (15 min from Selva) in the late afternoon — Sassolungo framed by golden larches. Zero effort, very high return.

Mon 12 Oct

The Great Dolomites Road — via Carezza and CanazeiFriends' picks ×2

Selva → Passo Sella → Canazei → Lago di Carezza → Passo Pordoi → Falzarego → Passo Tre Croci · ~150 km, ~4 h driving, no tolls

I've rerouted this day to pick up both Canazei and Lago di Carezza. You were driving to Cortina anyway; this version swings west first and comes back over the high passes, which turns a transfer into the best scenic drive in the Alps.

  • 08:00Leave Selva over Passo Sella (2,244 m) — Sassolungo filling the windscreen the whole way up. 20 minutes.
  • 09:30Lago di Carezza (Karersee, 1,534 m) — the emerald lake that reflects the Latemar. Paid pay-and-display car park on the SS241 with an underpass to the shore; the boardwalk loop takes 20–30 min. Go early: the reflection needs still water, and by late morning the coach parties arrive. Swimming and going down to the water's edge are banned — it's a viewing lake, not a swimming one.
  • 11:00Canazei (1,465 m), the main town of Val di Fassa — Ladin, lively, and the natural coffee-and-lunch stop of the day. The Sass Pordoi cable car above it is spectacular but its season almost certainly ends before you arrive; treat it as a bonus if it's spinning, not a plan.
  • 12:45Passo Pordoi (2,239 m) → Arabba → SS48 Passo Falzarego (2,105 m). Four high passes in one morning. Budget stopping time — you will keep stopping.
  • ~15:20Cortina → SR48 to Passo Tre Croci. Check in.
  • 19:30Dinner at El Brite de Larieto — an agriturismo with its own dairy, effectively on your doorstep on the Tre Croci road. Open until late October, closed Wednesdays. Book: +39 368 7008083.
Fallback plan — the slow morning. The 08:00 start costs you two things worth naming: Segra Sacun, the biggest traditional market of the year, in Ortisei 08:00–16:00 today (food stalls, crafts, and a tradition of men giving women a decorated pear), and a last walk up Vallunga from Selva — a flat gravel track up a glacier-carved valley under the Puez walls, 2.5 h return, larches the whole way.

If either of those appeals more than a scenic drive — or if you wake up to rain, when Carezza's reflection is pointless anyway — take the short pass route instead: Passo Gardena → Corvara → Passo Campolongo → Arabba → Falzarego. 85 km, 2h45, still one of the great drives, and it leaves you the whole morning free. You'd drop Lago di Carezza; Canazei you'd also miss, since it's on the Passo Sella side.
Bad-weather fallback: if it has snowed, abandon the passes entirely — down to Ponte Gardena, A22 north, Val Pusteria via Dobbiaco, then SS51 south. 145 km, ~2h20, actually faster on the clock, just far less beautiful. Check pass status that morning at venetostrade.it or the Bolzano traffic portal.

12–16 October · Cortina d'Ampezzo

Base: B&B Hotel Passo Tre Croci, 1,805 m. Expect 5–9 °C by day, hard frost most nights, 0–6 °C on the trails at 2,000–2,500 m. Sunrise ~07:26, sunset ~18:30 — and the pass loses direct sun much earlier. Be off exposed terrain by 17:00.

Three rules for this leg. (1) Carry lunch and water every single day — Locatelli, Scoiattoli, Nuvolau, Cinque Torri, Averau and Vandelli are all shut. (2) The only open lift is Lagazuoi, to 18 Oct. (3) Reorder the days below by the forecast — give Tre Cime your clearest day and Sorapis your mildest.
Tue 13 Oct

Lago di Braies at dawn, then Tre CimeFriends' picks ×2

The big one. ~07:00 to ~17:00, ~3 h driving, two of the most photographed places in the Alps.

07:00 — leave for Lago di Braies (Pragser Wildsee, 1,496 m). SR48 → Cortina → SS51 north → Dobbiaco → SS49 → Braies. ~55 km, about an hour. Sunrise is 07:26, so you'll arrive around 08:00 into the good light — your friend is right that before 9 is the whole trick. Still water for the reflection, the wooden boathouse empty, and the coach parties still an hour away.

The shore loop is 3.5 km, flat, about an hour. No reservation needed in October — the access-control system only runs 1 July–15 September. Parking is paid pay-and-display in the lots at the lakehead. The rowing boats are a summer thing and will most likely be off the water.

~09:45 — drive on to Tre Cime via Dobbiaco → Landro → Misurina, ~50 minutes. It's a clean loop; you're not backtracking.

Stop at Lago di Misurina on the way through (1,754 m) — it's directly on the road, ten minutes is enough. The classic view is the big belle-époque Grand Hotel Misurina reflected in the water with the Sorapis group behind. You'll also pass it again on the way home, so take whichever light is better. The Col de Varda chairlift above it closed on 20 September.

Tre Cime di Lavaredo — the loop 8.8–10 km, +425 m, 3–4 h · plus Cadini viewpoint 3.9 km, +144 m, 1–1.5 h

Book your slot at pass.auronzo.info, €40 per car, valid 12 hours; autumn access is daytime only, roughly 08:00–18:00. Book it for late morning. Toll gate at Misurina, then 5 km up to Rifugio Auronzo at 2,320 m, ~700 spaces.

Walk the loop anticlockwise: Cappella degli Alpini → Rifugio Lavaredo → Forcella Lavaredo (where the three north faces finally line up) → Rifugio Locatelli → Malga Langalm → back. Well graded, no exposure. Locatelli is closed, so there's no food, water or shelter for the middle two-thirds, and the wind at Forcella Lavaredo in October is brutal — take a proper shell.

Add the Cadini di Misurina viewpoint — an easy hour south on trail 117, ending on the famous ledge above a fin of jagged spires. The final spur is a narrow, exposed path on a rock nose. Dry conditions only; skip it if there's frost or fresh snow.

The bus from Dobbiaco stops running 11 October, so driving is the only option — another reason to book the slot early.
This is a 10-hour day. If that's too much on your first full day in Cortina, split it: do Tre Cime alone today, and put Braies on Wednesday morning before Lago Federa instead — Braies 08:00–09:30, drive back down, and you're at the Federa trailhead by 11:15 with plenty of day left for a 3-hour hike and a hut lunch. Costs you an extra 1h15 of driving but makes both days comfortable.

Wed 14 Oct

Lago Federa — the golden larches

From Ponte di Ru Curto · 8.4 km / +469 m / 3 h out-and-back, or 13.1 km / +700 m / 5 h circuit

The most famous larch location in the Dolomites, and mid-October is when the high stands turn. A small alpine lake at 2,046 m ringed with gold, the Croda da Lago wall behind it.

Rifugio Croda da Lago / Palmieri operates into the autumn and is the single most reliable hut lunch of your Cortina leg — but phone first: +39 0436 862085. If they're serving, this is the day you eat on a mountain.

El Brite is closed on Wednesdays — book dinner in Cortina instead. Baita Fraina (mountain-lodge cooking in small panelled rooms above town) or SanBrite if you want the splurge; see the eating section.
On the larches, honestly: peak at 1,900–2,300 m usually runs 8–20 October, valley floors later. You'll catch the start-to-middle of it — very good, probably not absolute peak. It shifts ±10 days a year with the first hard frosts.

Thu 15 Oct

Lagazuoi, Cinque Torri, home over Passo GiauBlog picks

Falzarego out, Giau back · a loop, not an out-and-back · ~25 min from the hotel

Morning: the Lagazuoi cable car from Passo Falzarego to 2,752 m — the last lift standing, running to 18 October. First up 09:00, every 15 min, last down 17:00. Lunch or coffee on the Rifugio Lagazuoi terrace with the Marmolada opposite is one of the great tables in the Alps. Call +39 0436 867303 to confirm they're serving.

Afternoon: park free at Col Gallina, five minutes down the road, and walk the Cinque Torri / Averau / Nuvolau circuit — 9.6 km, +600 m, ~3h45, via Lago di Limedes. The chairlift and all three huts are closed, so carry food. The payoff is the Cinque Torri open-air WWI museum: restored trenches and tunnels among the five towers, and larch forest with Lagazuoi and Tofana di Rozes behind it. The final ridge to Nuvolau is narrow with a cable — mild exposure, dry conditions only.

Come home over Passo Giau (2,236 m) instead of back the way you came. From Col Gallina, continue west a few minutes and turn south on the SP638 — Giau, then down to Cortina, making the whole day a loop rather than an out-and-back for no extra time. It's the most dramatic of all the Cortina passes: a bare amphitheatre under Ra Gusela and Nuvolau, and larch stands that in mid-October are as good as Lago Federa's. Rifugio Fedare at the top is open year-round if you want a late coffee. The pass stays open through winter, so October is never a problem.
If you'd rather do one big thing than two: take the cable car up and walk down through Val Travenanzes, a wild hanging valley, to Ponte Outo on the SR48 — 4–5 h, mostly descent, with some cabled steps near the forcella. It needs a car shuttle, so only if you can arrange a lift back to Falzarego. Alternatively, Passo Giau → Monte Mondeval is 12.8 km, ~5 h, moderate, open and panoramic, with larches most of the way — a full-day substitute for the Falzarego pair.

Lago di Sorapis — swap it in on your mildest day

Trail 215, straight from the hotel · 11.5 km out-and-back, ~500 m, ~4 h

The trailhead is at Passo Tre Croci — you walk out of the hotel and start. A milky turquoise glacial lake under the Sorapis walls, and one of the most photographed places in the Dolomites.

Read this before you commit. Trail 215 has narrow exposed ledge traverses secured by steel cables, metal ladders and stairs, and polished rock with poor grip. Those sections face north and east and sit in shade — iced ladders and verglas are a genuine October risk. Rifugio Vandelli closed on 20 September, so there's no water, shelter or toilet. The lake also shrinks to a pool by October and can be partly frozen. Go on a dry, mild day, start mid-morning once the sun has been on it, carry microspikes, and turn back if there's ice. Ask the hotel the evening before — they'll know the ladder conditions.

This is the natural hike to drop if the weather doesn't cooperate, and the natural one to swap in for Lago Federa or Cinque Torri if it does.

Passo Tre Croci → Venice · ~170 km · 3h–3h30 · toll ~€8.20

16–17 October · Venice

Fri 16 Oct

One night, done properly

Stay on the island. Dorsoduro or Cannaregio.

Where to stay. Dorsoduro (Accademia / Zattere) is the romantic pick — the Zattere at sunset with a spritz is the memory you're buying. Cannaregio is the easy pick: one vaporetto stop from Piazzale Roma, the best cicchetti crawl in the city, genuinely local. Skip San Marco and skip Castello — Castello is lovely for three nights and miserable with luggage for one.

Do not stay in Mestre. It saves you €4–14 and costs you the only thing that makes a one-night Venice worth doing: the city between 18:00 and 10:00, when the day-trippers are gone and the calli are empty and lit.
  • 15:30Drop bags. Walk the Zattere — the long sunny quay facing Giudecca. Punta della Dogana, the Salute.
  • 18:00Cannaregio bacaro crawl along Fondamenta della Misericordia and Ormesini. Osteria alla Vedova for the legendary deep-fried polpetta. Vino Vero for natural wine and crostini with stracciatella and anchovy. Al Timon, where everyone sits on the canal edge and on the moored boat. €2–5 a cicchetto, €2–3 an ombra. Carry cash.
  • 20:30Dinner. Antiche Carampane (San Polo) — the one with the sign in the window saying no pizza, no lasagne, no tourist menu. Outstanding fried fish. ~€70–90 pp, book weeks ahead, closed Sun–Mon so Friday works. Alternatives: Osteria alle Testiere (22 seats, pure lagoon fish) or Local (Michelin-starred, elevated cicchetti).
  • 22:30Vaporetto Line 1, the whole Grand Canal, after dark. An hour, all stops, already paid for. Nothing else in Venice is this good for free.
October is moeche season — soft-shell lagoon crabs, fried whole, available for only a few weeks a year. If you see them on a menu, order them without thinking about it.
No access fee. The 2026 trial ran 3 April–26 July and is over. Hotel tourist tax is ~€4.50 pp per night at a 4★, paid at checkout.

Sat 17 Oct

Rialto, then west to Garda

Venice → Lake Garda south shore · 153 km · 1h45–2h · toll €11.10

  • 09:00Rialto market — Saturday morning is the good one. Fish, produce, noise.
  • 11:00All'Arco, Calle dell'Arco, San Polo 436. A standing-room counter, no written menu, everything made that morning — baccalà mantecato, sarde in saor, raw crudi. €1.50–4 each. No reservations, closed Sunday, and the shutters come down around 14:00 when the trays are empty. Go early.
  • 13:30Collect the car at Tronchetto. A4 west to Sirmione or Desenzano.
  • ~16:00Check in at Garda. Evening: the Salò lakefront passeggiata, or Sirmione's old town once the day-trippers have gone — which, at this hour in October, they have.
If you stay in Sirmione: the historic centre peninsula is a 24/7 camera-controlled ZTL, year-round. Email your plate to the hotel at least 24–48 h ahead and keep the written confirmation. Doing it verbally at check-in is too late — the camera has already fired. Fines are €80–100 plus a rental admin fee, arriving 6–14 months later.

Sun 18 Oct

Sirmione, Bardolino, and a love-knot

Sunday is the constraint — most wineries are Mon–Sat

  • 09:30Sirmione — the Castello Scaligero with its water moat, then the 2 km walk out to the Grotte di Catullo at the tip of the peninsula: a vast Roman villa ruin in an olive grove with water on three sides. Best early, before the coaches.
  • 12:30Lunch at Valeggio sul Mincio, 15 min from Peschiera — home of tortellini di Valeggio, the "nodo d'amore", love-knot tortellini, with their own legend about a nymph and a knight. About as on-theme as a honeymoon lunch gets.
  • 15:00Guerrieri Rizzardi in Bardolino — an aristocratic estate that does tastings daily 10:00–18:30, one of the few open on a Sunday. From ~€15 for 90 minutes. Book: visite@guerrieri-rizzardi.it. Nearby, Cantina Zeni's wine museum is free and needs no booking.
  • 17:00Back to the hotel spa, or the Museo dell'Olio d'Oliva at Cisano — free, open daily, and mid-October is the start of the Garda DOP olive harvest.
  • 20:00Dinner. Splurge: Esplanade in Desenzano (★, lakefront) or Tancredi in Sirmione (★, a garden that seems to enter the lake). Simpler and excellent: Il Giardino delle Esperidi in Bardolino (700 labels, closed Tuesdays) or Osteria al Torcol in Sirmione.
Drink: Lugana — the serious white of this shore, made from Turbiana, and it ages. Then Bardolino and its pale Chiaretto rosé. Eat: luccio in salsa (pike with capers and anchovy), coregone, and Garda DOP olive oil on everything.

Mon 19 Oct

Franciacorta, then home

Garda → MXP · 165 km · 2h00–2h30 · flight 23:00

You have a whole day. Use it, but keep the buffer.

  • 10:00Zenato, Peschiera — Lugana and Amarone under one roof, and the best single tasting on the lake. Classic tour €40 (four wines with cheese, salumi and oil, 90 min). Mon–Sat, booking essential.
  • 13:00Check out, then head west on the A4 into Franciacorta — Italy's best metodo classico sparkling wine, ~40 min along your route. Ca' del Bosco or Bellavista, and lunch among the vines. It turns dead driving time into the last good thing you do.
  • 17:00Leave Franciacorta. The A4 between Brescia and Milan on a Monday afternoon is the least predictable stretch of the whole trip.
  • ~19:45Be at the rental return lot by 20:00, not 21:00. Allow 30–45 min for the return level, the walk-around inspection, key handover and the walk to departures.
  • 20:30Check in. For a 23:00 non-Schengen departure you want to be landside by 20:00–20:30. Security at T1 after 20:00 is one of the quietest windows of the day, ~15 min.
Two things that will bite if you ignore them. Confirm the rental returns at the same terminal you fly from — most companies return at T1, and if you're departing from T2 you need to add 20–30 min for the shuttle. And whoever drives does not taste; two cellar visits in a day and a 0.5 g/l limit do not mix. Split the tasting: one of you drives to Franciacorta, the other drives home.
