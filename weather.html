Bad weather — a plan B for every day

The one rule that matters more than any single fallback

  1. Reorder within each block, don't push through. You have four days in Val Gardena and four in Cortina. The plan below assigns them in a sensible default order — but the moment the forecast disagrees, swap. Give Seceda, the coffin gondola and Tre Cime your clear days and let the low, forested, indoor things absorb the bad ones. Following the itinerary blindly is the only way to waste this trip's weather.
  2. Never pre-buy a view. Seceda's cable car (€74), the Lagazuoi cable car and the Tre Cime toll slot (€40) are all money spent on a view that cloud can erase entirely. Look out of the window first. Book Tre Cime only when the forecast is firm, ~3–4 days out.
  3. Rain at 200 m is snow at 2,000 m. A wet day on Lake Como is a mildly annoying day. The same front over Passo Tre Croci can close a trail. Check the mountain forecast, not the valley one.

Lake Como · 5–8 October

Mon 5 Oct — arrival

Nothing to salvage — it's a drive

RainNo change of plan, just of pace. The SS36 at night in heavy rain is genuinely tiring after a flight — add 20–30 minutes and don't try to make up time. This is another argument for eating at the airport rather than hunting for a kitchen at 22:15 in the wet.
Hard coldIrrelevant at 250 m in early October. Just have one warm layer accessible in the cabin bag rather than buried in the hold.

Tue 6 Oct — west shore

What breaks: Balbianello is a garden on a promontory, and the taxi-boat may not run

BreaksVilla del Balbianello's whole appeal is outdoors, the woodland approach path turns slippery, and the Lido di Lenno taxi-boat can be suspended in bad weather — leaving you a 25-minute walk in the rain each way.
RainSwap the day for Como town, which is almost entirely indoors. The Duomo — one of the great late-Gothic/Renaissance hybrids in Italy, free. The Museo della Seta (silk museum): Como was the silk capital of Europe and this is a far better museum than it sounds. The Tempio Voltiano. Basilica di Sant'Abbondio, Romanesque, quiet, empty. Skip the Brunate funicular — you'd be paying to sit inside a cloud.

If you'd rather stay on plan: Villa Carlotta still works — its interior holds real art (Canova, Hayez), so it's the one villa that survives a wet day. Balbianello's 45-minute guided interior tour is also indoors, if you're willing to get to the door.
Hard coldCold and clear is a gift here — October cold snaps are when the haze clears and you can see Monte Rosa from the ridges. Nothing needs changing. Wrap up for the ferry deck.

Wed 7 Oct — Varenna

What breaks: the Sentiero del Viandante is polished limestone

BreaksThe Viandante's paving is genuinely slippery wet — this is the one Lake Como walk where rain is a safety issue rather than a comfort issue. Don't do it in a downpour.
RainSwap Wednesday and Thursday: go to Bergamo today. The Accademia Carrara is a first-rate picture gallery — Botticelli, Bellini, Raphael, Titian — and Città Alta's arcaded streets, Santa Maria Maggiore and the Cappella Colleoni are all covered or indoors. It's the strongest wet-weather day available on this whole leg, and moving it here costs you nothing because Thursday's Bergamo stop was optional anyway.

Staying local instead: Villa Monastero's house-museum (furnished period rooms, indoors), then Lecco — Villa Manzoni and Palazzo delle Paure, €6 each. The Orrido di Bellano gorge is arguably better with more water in it, but ⚠️ it can close on high water — check before driving over.
Hard coldIdeal Viandante conditions. It's a south-facing track and you generate plenty of heat climbing out of Varenna. Go.

Thu 8 Oct — transfer to Val Gardena

What breaks: only the optional Monte Barro viewpoint

RainDrop Monte Barro — it's a panorama, and there'd be no panorama. Leave earlier, spend longer in Bergamo indoors (Accademia Carrara, Santa Maria Maggiore), and have a proper long lunch before the A22. The driving itself is motorway and unaffected, though add 30 minutes for a wet A4/A22.
Hard coldFine. One thing to watch: if a cold front has come through, check the SS242 up Val Gardena for early snow before you commit to the last hour. The A22 exit at Chiusa is at 520 m; Selva is at 1,563 m.

Val Gardena · 8–12 October

The Val Gardena block's saving grace: Bolzano is only ~50 minutes down the valley, and it holds the single best wet-weather destination of the entire trip — the South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology, home of Ötzi the Iceman, the 5,300-year-old mummy found in a glacier up the road. If one of these four days is a write-off, that's where it goes.

Fri 9 Oct — Seceda

What breaks: everything. This is the most weather-dependent day of the trip.

BreaksSeceda is one view. In cloud you pay €74 (or the Col Raiser fare) to stand in fog. There is no partial credit.
RainMove Seceda to another day in the block — you have four and only need one clear one. Then: drive to Bolzano for Ötzi, the arcaded Portici, and the Duomo. Or stay in the valley for Vallunga — a flat gravel track up a forested glacial valley, low, sheltered and genuinely fine in drizzle, 2.5 h return from Selva. Ortisei also has the Museum Gherdëina, on Ladin culture and the valley's woodcarving tradition — ⚠️ check October hours, they're seasonal.
Hard coldCold and clear is the best possible Seceda day — sharpest air, no haze, and the crowds thin further. Go early, take gloves and a hat for the ridge, and expect the ground to be frozen hard rather than muddy, which actually makes the walking better.

Sat 10 Oct — Val di Funes + Törggelen

Half of this day is already weather-proof

BreaksOnly the morning. The Adolf Munkel Weg is a walk beneath a rock wall you won't be able to see, and the Santa Maddalena viewpoint is purely a photograph.
RainTörggelen is indoors and unaffected — keep it, and just extend the lunch. For the morning, swap the hike for Abbazia di Novacella / Neustift near Bressanone: a working Augustinian monastery with a famous rotunda library, a baroque church, and its own winery doing tastings. Historic, indoors, and wine — it's arguably a better rainy morning than the hike is a sunny one. Or wander Bressanone/Brixen (Duomo with frescoed cloister, Hofburg palace, arcaded streets) or tiny Chiusa/Klausen.
Hard coldTörggelen is the cold-weather tradition — chestnuts, a wood stove and new wine exist precisely for this. Keep everything; wear more.

Sun 11 Oct — coffin gondola + Alpe di Siusi

What breaks: both halves — and this one you can't reschedule

BreaksBe straight about this: if Sunday is a washout, the coffin gondola is simply lost. It closes today, there is no other day, and a small exposed gondola can also be held for high wind in clear weather. Alpe di Siusi in cloud is a grey field.
RainGo to Terme Merano — around 1h15 away, a large thermal complex with indoor and outdoor pools and a big sauna world. On a cold wet Sunday on your honeymoon this is not a consolation prize; it's arguably better than the plan. Merano itself has covered arcades and the Kurhaus if you want a town afterwards.

Cheaper and closer: Bolzano and Ötzi, or the hotel's own wellness area and a very long lunch. ⚠️ Ortisei has a public pool and sauna complex too — check it's open in the shoulder season before driving over.
Hard coldCold and clear is fine for both, and the meadow is at its best under low golden light. Wind is the thing that stops the gondola, not cold — if it's blowing hard, ring Passo Sella before driving up.

Mon 12 Oct — Great Dolomites Road

What breaks: the entire point of the routing

BreaksCarezza's reflection needs still water and light; four high passes in cloud are just four hours of grey hairpins; and after snow the passes become a genuine driving problem.
RainTake the valley route — Ponte Gardena → A22 → Val Pusteria → Dobbiaco → SS51. 145 km, ~2h20, faster and safer, and it opens a good indoor stop: Brunico/Bruneck, a handsome market town whose castle houses the Messner Mountain Museum Ripa, on the mountain peoples of the world. ⚠️ Check its October hours. Or take the Segra Sacun fallback and have a slow Ortisei morning — though a market in heavy rain is a thin experience, so judge it on the day.
Hard coldClear and cold is the ideal day for this drive — but check the passes at first light. Falzarego is 2,105 m and Pordoi 2,239 m; overnight ice on hairpins is the real hazard, not snow depth. Check venetostrade.it and the Bolzano traffic portal before you leave, and keep the valley route in your pocket.

Cortina · 12–16 October

Be honest about Cortina in the wet: its indoor offering is thin — a couple of small museums and a nice shopping street. The strongest wet-weather move from Passo Tre Croci is to drive an hour north into Val Pusteria, where San Candido, Dobbiaco and Brunico all have proper indoor options and a pool complex. Plan for that rather than hoping Cortina town will fill a day.

Tue 13 Oct — Braies + Tre Cime

What breaks: Tre Cime completely. Braies, surprisingly little.

BreaksTre Cime in cloud is €40 for nothing, and Forcella Lavaredo in driving rain with no open refuge is genuinely unpleasant. Braies, though, is a lake in a forest — mist and drizzle suit it, and it's arguably more atmospheric moody than sunny.
RainDo Braies anyway, then bail north instead of east. San Candido/Innichen is 20 minutes on — a beautiful Romanesque collegiate church and Acquafun, an indoor pool and wellness centre (⚠️ check hours). Dobbiaco/Toblach has the Gustav Mahler connection and its cultural centre. Save the €40 and book the Tre Cime slot for Wednesday or Thursday instead — this is exactly why you don't book it early.
Hard coldPerfect for both, and hard frost means Braies will be still as glass at 08:00. The one thing to respect is wind chill at Forcella Lavaredo, which is brutal in October — full shell, gloves, hat, and don't linger at the col.

Wed 14 Oct — Lago Federa

The most weather-tolerant hike on the Cortina leg

BreaksLittle, and here's the counterintuitive bit: overcast light is genuinely good for larches. Flat light saturates the gold instead of blowing it out, so a grey day photographs better than harsh sun. The real problem is mud and a wet, slippery descent.
RainIn drizzle, go anyway — take the shorter 8.4 km out-and-back rather than the circuit, and hope Rifugio Palmieri is serving. In steady rain, retreat to Cortina town: the Museo Paleontologico Rinaldo Zardini (Dolomite fossils — these mountains are a fossilised coral reef, which is a good thing to learn while looking at them) and the Mario Rimoldi modern art collection, both in the Ciasa de ra Regoles. ⚠️ Small municipal museums, so check October hours. Then Corso Italia and a long lunch. El Brite is closed today, so book somewhere in town regardless.
Hard coldIdeal. Frozen ground beats mud, and the larches are triggered by exactly this. Expect the lake to have ice at the edges.

Thu 15 Oct — Lagazuoi, Cinque Torri, Passo Giau

What breaks: the cable car, and the open-air museum

BreaksLagazuoi is a 2,752 m viewpoint — pointless in cloud, and the cable car can be held for wind. The Cinque Torri WWI trenches are outdoors and unpleasant wet. Passo Giau's whole appeal is the view from it.
RainKeep the theme, move it indoors. The Museo della Grande Guerra at Forte Tre Sassi, a restored Austro-Hungarian fort at Passo Valparola just above Falzarego, covers exactly the WWI front you'd have been walking through at Cinque Torri and Lagazuoi — and it has a roof. ⚠️ Check October hours; it's seasonal. Follow it with a long lunch at Rifugio Ospitale on the SS51, an eleventh-century hospice turned restaurant, road-accessible and open in shoulder season.

If it's a total washout, this is the day to drive north to Val Pusteria instead.
Hard coldExcellent for all three — and Passo Giau's larches want exactly this. Note the cable car's last down is 17:00; in cold clear weather the light tempts you to stay too long at 2,752 m. Don't miss it.

Fri 16 Oct — transfer to Venice

A driving day, so weather costs time rather than content

RainAdd 30 minutes — the SS51 down the Cadore is slower wet. Otherwise no change, and a wet transfer day is the cheapest possible day to lose. If anything, a bad forecast today is good news: it means you can be more aggressive about spending clear days on the mountains earlier in the week.
Hard coldWatch for overnight ice on the SR48 down from Passo Tre Croci at 1,805 m — the first few kilometres are the coldest of the whole drive. Leave a little later rather than earlier.

Venice & Lake Garda · 16–19 October

Fri 16 – Sat 17 Oct — Venice

The most rain-proof stop of the trip — but read the acqua alta note

BreaksAlmost nothing. Bacaro crawling is indoors by definition, and Venice in rain is atmospheric rather than ruined. The real October risk isn't rain, it's acqua alta — the high-water season starts now and runs to January. The MOSE barriers prevent serious flooding these days, but St Mark's Square still floods at lower thresholds.
RainCheck the city's tide forecast the night before (the Comune di Venezia publishes it; there are apps too) and pack or buy rubber overshoes — every shop sells them. Then simply go indoors: the Gallerie dell'Accademia and the Peggy Guggenheim Collection are both a few minutes from a Dorsoduro hotel, and the Scuola Grande di San Rocco — Tintoretto's entire cycle, in the room he painted it for — is one of the great interiors in Europe and never crowded. The Grand Canal after dark from a vaporetto is better in rain, with the palazzo lights smearing on the water.
Hard coldVenice in cold, still, clear October weather is close to the best it ever is — empty, sharp, and the light is extraordinary. No changes at all, beyond a proper coat for standing on a vaporetto deck.

Sun 18 Oct — Lake Garda

What breaks: only the Roman ruin. Everything else has a roof.

BreaksThe Grotte di Catullo is an open ruin in an olive grove — genuinely miserable in rain, and the whole point is the setting.
RainThis is the day the thermal spa earns its place. Aquaria Thermal SPA in Sirmione sits on a sulphurous thermal spring with indoor and outdoor pools — being outside in hot water in cold rain is one of the better feelings available, and it's a five-minute walk from the castle.

Everything else on the day already has a roof: the Guerrieri Rizzardi tasting, the free olive oil museum at Cisano, and the tortellini lunch at Valeggio. And Verona is 30 minutes away with the Arena, Castelvecchio and the Duomo — just mind the ZTL (Mon–Fri 10:00–13:30 and 16:00–18:00, Sat–Sun 10:00–13:30) and park at Cittadella.
Hard coldGarda is the mildest place you'll be all trip — it grows lemons and olives for a reason. Cold here means a jumper, not a change of plan. The Grotte in cold clear air are excellent and empty.

Mon 19 Oct — departure

Weather affects only your buffer

RainCellars are indoors, so Zenato and Franciacorta are unaffected — the plan stands. But the A4 between Brescia and Milan in Monday-afternoon rain is the least predictable stretch of the entire trip. Leave Franciacorta by 16:00 instead of 17:00 and absorb the extra time airside, where there's a bar.
Hard coldNo effect on the Lombard plain. Fog is the thing to watch, not cold — the Po valley produces genuinely thick autumn fog that can halve motorway speeds. Same answer: leave earlier.
Three things to pack specifically for the bad days: a proper waterproof shell each (not a showerproof jacket — you may be out for hours), microspikes for frozen or icy trail sections, and a dry pair of shoes left in the car so a wet hike doesn't cost you the evening. ⚠️ Museum and small-attraction hours in this list are shoulder-season and I couldn't verify several of them — anything marked with a warning triangle deserves a two-minute check before you drive there.
