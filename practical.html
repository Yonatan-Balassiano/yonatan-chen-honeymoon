Parking — how it works, and where to leave the car

The colour code is the whole game. Once you know it you can park anywhere in Italy without thinking.

Line colourMeansWhat to do
WhiteFreeJust park. Check for a small sign adding a time limit or a disco orario requirement.
BluePaidPay at the meter or by app. Enforcement is usually ~08:00–20:00; outside those hours it's often free — read the sign.
YellowResidents / disabled onlyNever. This is where tourists get towed.

Four habits that save you money and arguments:

  • Install EasyPark or MooneyGo before you fly. Both work on blue lines across Lombardy and let you extend remotely instead of running back to the car. Many older lakeside meters are coin-only, so carry €1 and €2 coins as backup.
  • Set the satnav to the car park, never the destination. This single habit prevents every ZTL fine on this trip.
  • Walk uphill to save money. On Lake Como the price gradient is brutal and short — lakefront lots are blue-line paid, and the white-line streets one terrace up the hill are free. Ten minutes of walking is usually the whole difference.
  • October helps you. Several lakeside lots shorten their paid hours or drop rates once the season ends in early October. Always read the current sign rather than assuming the summer rate.

On the rates above: the free park-and-ride sites in Bergamo and the ATB garage capacities are confirmed from the operator. The Lake Como hourly figures are estimates — every municipal site on the lake blocks automated access, so I couldn't pull official 2026 tariffs. Treat them as "expect roughly this", read the meter, and don't be surprised by ±50%. The free options and the walk-uphill principle are the reliable part.
If you end up in Como town or Bellagio: both are the same shape — a paid lakefront ring and free white lines further back or uphill. In Como aim for the Val Mulini / Via Bellinzona area behind the centre rather than the waterfront. In Bellagio the lakefront is ZTL and the signed lots above and beside the centre are paid; the free spaces start on the road out towards Loppia and San Giovanni, about 12 minutes' walk in.
Parking for the friends' picks: Lago di Carezza and Lago di Braies both have paid pay-and-display lots at the lakehead — Braies needs no reservation in October, since that system only runs 1 July–15 September. Zannes in Val di Funes may require an online parking booking in 2026 — check before you drive up, and remember non-resident cars can't reach the Santa Maddalena viewpoint at all. Alpe di Siusi you don't park at: take the cableway from Siusi. Tre Cime is the €40 pre-booked toll road. Canazei has ordinary paid town lots. Everything else in the Dolomites — Vallunga, Ponte di Ru Curto, Col Gallina, Passo Falzarego, Passo Sella — is free or cheap pay-and-display.

Driving rules that save money:

  • Never let the satnav route you into a historic centre. Set the destination to a named car park, not the hotel. Hard ZTLs on your route: Cortina, Ortisei, Sirmione, Bellagio, Varenna, Bergamo, Verona.
  • If you realise you've entered a ZTL, don't reverse or loop — every pass under the camera is a separate fine.
  • Toll lanes: yellow "T" is Telepass only — never enter it. Blue takes cards, white/green takes cash, mixed staffed lanes take everything.
  • Ask at pickup whether a Telepass transponder is pre-installed — some companies auto-charge tolls at a 20–35% markup.
  • Fuel: ~€1.83/l petrol. Self-service is €0.15–0.20/l cheaper than servito, and motorway stations are the most expensive. Return the car full.
Winter tyres. Not legally required on your dates — the Bolzano and Belluno obligations start 15 November. But I could not read the text of Veneto Strade's separate passi dolomitici ordinance, which could differ, and regardless: temporary signage can mandate chains immediately after any snowfall, at any time of year, and the Carabinieri do enforce it on the passes. Ask for M+S / all-season tyres at booking, in writing, and check the sidewall at pickup. Re-check venetostrade.it the week before you travel.

Bring: proper hiking boots with grip (Como's limestone and the Dolomite ledges are both slippery wet) · waterproof shell · insulated mid-layer · gloves, hat, poles · microspikes — light, cheap, and the difference between doing Sorapis and abandoning it · sunglasses · one warm layer for lakeside evenings, which turn cold fast after sunset · cash for Venice bacari, several of which are cash-only.

Daylight: sunset moves from 18:58 on 5 Oct to 18:20 on 19 Oct. Daylight saving doesn't end until 25 October, so you keep the long evenings the whole trip. Golden hour on the lakes is roughly 17:45–18:55; be off exposed mountain ground by 17:00.
