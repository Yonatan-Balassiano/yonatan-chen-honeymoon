Friends' picks — all seven, and where each one lands

Good list — your friends know the Dolomites. Three were already the backbone of the plan (Seceda, Alpe di Siusi, Tre Cime), which is a decent sanity check. The other four are now slotted in, and two of them changed the shape of a day for the better.

1 · Seceda

Val Gardena · 2,518 m · cable car from Ortisei, runs to 2 November

The tilted blade of rock that is the single most recognisable view in the Dolomites. Ridge walk to Rifugio Firenze, back down via Col Raiser — 8.9 km, +575 m, 3–3.5 h. Rifugio Firenze closes 12 October so Friday is your window for the hut lunch.

✓ Already the plan — Fri 9 Oct

2 · Val di Funes

Villnöss · ~50 min from Selva · the other side of the Odle

Newly added. The Adolf Munkel Weg runs along the base of the Odle north wall — 9.2 km circuit, +378 m, 3–3.5 h, easy to moderate, from the Zannes car park. Plus the church of Santa Maddalena, the most photographed building in South Tyrol.

Two access catches: non-resident cars are barred from the Santa Maddalena panorama viewpoint May–November (park in the village, walk 20–30 min), and the Zannes car park is pre-bookable online in 2026 — check before you drive up.

It pairs perfectly with Törggelen, which is 15 minutes away at Villandro. Hike in the morning, farm table at 13:30.

Added — Sat 10 Oct, morning

3 · Alpe di Siusi

Seiser Alm · 1,800–2,000 m · Europe's largest high-altitude meadow

Rolling golden pasture with Sassolungo and the Sciliar on either side. Up to 14.4 km, +483 m, 4.5 h, easy — the softest walking of the trip and a good recovery day. Take the Seiser Alm Bahn from Siusi (€30 return); don't drive up — the road is closed to non-resident cars 09:00–17:00 and parking needs an online reservation.

✓ Already the plan — Sun 11 Oct

4 · Lago di Carezza

Karersee · 1,534 m · SS241, ~35 min west of Canazei

Newly added, and it reshaped your transfer day. The emerald lake that mirrors the Latemar. The visit itself is short — a 20–30 minute boardwalk loop from a paid car park with an underpass to the shore — but the reflection is worth engineering the morning around.

Go early: you need still water, and the coaches arrive late morning. Going down to the water's edge and swimming are both banned; it's a viewing lake.

It's a dead-end detour from anywhere except the Great Dolomites Road, which is why it now sits on your Cortina transfer day rather than eating a Val Gardena hiking day.

Added — Mon 12 Oct, ~09:30

5 · Canazei

Val di Fassa · 1,465 m · over Passo Sella, ~35 min from Selva

Newly added. The main town of the Ladin Val di Fassa — lively, walkable, and the natural coffee-and-lunch stop between Carezza and Passo Pordoi. It sits at the hub of the four great passes, which is exactly why it works as the midpoint of the transfer day.

The Sass Pordoi cable car above the pass is a spectacular ride to 2,950 m, but its season almost certainly ends before 12 October. If it's spinning, take it; don't plan on it.

Added — Mon 12 Oct, ~11:00

6 · Lago di Braies — before 09:00

Pragser Wildsee · 1,496 m · ~1 h north of Passo Tre Croci

Newly added, and your friend's timing advice is exactly right. Sunrise on 13 October is 07:26. Leave the hotel at 07:00, arrive around 08:00: still water for the reflection, the wooden boathouse empty, and the coach parties still an hour out. By 10:30 it's a different, much worse place.

Shore loop 3.5 km, flat, about an hour. No reservation needed in October — the access-control system only runs 1 July–15 September. Paid parking at the lakehead. Rowing boats will most likely be off the water.

Braies and Tre Cime are both north of you and connect cleanly via Dobbiaco and Misurina, so they make one loop rather than two separate drives.

Added — Tue 13 Oct, 08:00

7 · Tre Cime di Lavaredo

2,320–2,455 m · ~30 min from Passo Tre Croci via Misurina

The loop is 8.8–10 km, +425 m, 3–4 h, well graded and unexposed. Add the Cadini di Misurina viewpoint, an easy hour more.

The one thing that has changed since your friends went: the toll road now requires a pre-booked online slot at pass.auronzo.info, €40 per car with your licence plate, valid 12 hours. You can't just turn up and pay at the gate. Also: Rifugio Locatelli is closed, so there's no food, water or shelter on the far side of the loop.

✓ Already the plan — Tue 13 Oct
Net effect on the plan: Saturday gained a morning hike in Val di Funes, the Monday transfer became the Great Dolomites Road instead of the short pass route (~4 h driving instead of 2h45, and you lose the Segra Sacun market in Ortisei), and Tuesday now starts at 07:00 instead of 09:00. Nothing was dropped — but Tuesday is a genuine 10-hour day, so there's a split option on that card if you'd rather not.

Blog list — reviewed, one by one

Your blog list is good — most of it was already in the plan, which is reassuring. Four things are newly added, two are now fallbacks, and three items need correcting: two of them describe a lift that will be closed, and one is simply too far away to reach on this trip. Details below.

Newly added — four things that changed a day

"Coffin gondola" — Forcella Sassolungo, Passo Sella

2,240 → 2,681 m · two-person standing cabins that never stop

You flagged this as "need to go", and you were right to — but it nearly slipped through the plan entirely. The lift closes 11 October 2026. You drive over Passo Sella on the 12th on your way to Cortina, one day too late. So it has moved to Sunday morning, ahead of Alpe di Siusi, which now runs in the afternoon from the Ortisei side instead.

Five minutes up to Rifugio Toni Demetz, wedged in the notch between the Sassolungo and the Cinque Dita. Unnerving, brief, and completely out of proportion to the effort.

The 11 October date is single-sourced — verify before you build the morning on it.

Added — Sun 11 Oct, 09:30 · date-critical

Passo Giau

2,236 m · SP638, between Falzarego and Cortina

Newly added, and it costs nothing. Come home from the Lagazuoi / Cinque Torri day over Giau instead of retracing the Falzarego road — same amount of time, but the day becomes a loop. A bare amphitheatre under Ra Gusela and Nuvolau, with larch stands that in mid-October rival Lago Federa's.

Rifugio Fedare at the top is open year-round. The pass itself is kept open through winter, so October is never in doubt. If you want a full day out of it instead, Passo Giau → Monte Mondeval is 12.8 km, ~5 h, moderate, open and panoramic.

Added — Thu 15 Oct, return leg

Lago di Misurina

1,754 m · directly on the road to Tre Cime

Newly added as a ten-minute stop, because it's literally on your way and you'll pass it twice on the 13th. The shot is the belle-époque Grand Hotel Misurina reflected in the water with the Sorapis group behind. Take whichever of the two passes has the better light.

The Col de Varda chairlift above it closed on 20 September, so there's nothing to go up.

Added — Tue 13 Oct, en route

The cheaper Seceda route — corrected version

Santa Cristina → Col Raiser gondola → Pieralongia → the ridge

Your instinct is right and the €74 cable car is avoidable — but not the way those blogs describe. See the correction below. The version that works in October: Col Raiser gondola (runs to 1 Nov, materially cheaper, a fraction of the traffic) to ~2,106 m, then walk the section Fermeda used to cover — roughly +400 m, 1h15–1h30 via the Pieralongia towers — and come down past Rifugio Firenze for lunch.

Same ridge, same view, less money, far fewer people, one extra hour of climbing. For a honeymoon I'd take this over the crowded cable car.

Added as the preferred option — Fri 9 Oct

Needs correcting

Both blog routes via the Fermeda chairlift

"Col Raiser → Fermeda → ridge → Pieralongia" and "Cristauta/Praplan → trail 4/A1 → Fermeda"

The Fermeda chairlift runs 20 June – 20 September 2026. On 9 October it will have been shut for nearly three weeks. Both of those routes are describing a summer trip and neither works as written.

Col Raiser itself is fine (open to 1 November) — it's only the Fermeda leg that's gone, and you can walk it. The Cristauta / Praplan start is still a real route but becomes a +900–1,000 m climb to the ridge with no shortcut. Doable, but a big first day in the mountains.

Superseded — see the corrected version above

Molveno

Brenta Dolomites, Trentino · ~2 h from Selva, ~2 h from Cortina

Beautiful, and not reachable on this trip. Molveno is in the Brenta — a separate massif west of Trento, not part of the Dolomites you're staying in. There's no day on your itinerary where it costs less than four hours of driving.

It's really a casualty of your flight. From the north end of Lake Garda it's a natural 1h15 day trip — but you need the south-west shore for the Malpensa run, which puts Molveno back out of range. Worth remembering for a Garda trip another time.

Skip this trip

Fallbacks and optional

Segra Sacun market, Ortisei

Mon 12 October, 08:00–16:00 · the biggest traditional market of the year

Now written up as the explicit fallback for Monday. It clashes head-on with the 08:00 start for the Great Dolomites Road, so you can't have both. Food stalls, crafts, and a tradition of men giving women a decorated pear.

Take the fallback if: you'd rather have a slow last morning in the valley than a scenic drive, or you wake up to rain — in which case Carezza's reflection is pointless anyway and the short pass route (85 km, 2h45) is the better call.

Fallback — Mon 12 Oct

Geisler Alm / Rifugio delle Odle

Val di Funes, directly on the Adolf Munkel Weg

I'd push back gently on the "no go". You walk past its door whether you stop or not, so looking costs nothing. Its reputation for being busy and commercial is a high-summer problem; on a Saturday in mid-October that largely evaporates.

The real risk is that it's simply shut — I couldn't verify its October season. Treat it as a bonus if the door's open, and carry lunch either way.

Optional — Sat 10 Oct, en route
